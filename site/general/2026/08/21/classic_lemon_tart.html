Classic Lemon Tart

Classic Lemon Tart

A crisp sweet pastry shell filled with a silky, sharp lemon custard — baked low and slow so it sets without cracking.

Serves: 8 Tin: 23cm loose-bottomed tart tin

Ingredients### For the pastry

  • 175g plain flour
  • 75g icing sugar
  • 100g cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 1 tbsp cold water
  • ¼ tsp fine salt

For the filling

  • 4 large eggs
  • 150g caster sugar
  • 150ml lemon juice, freshly squeezed (about 4 lemons)
  • Zest of 2 lemons
  • 150ml double cream

Method

1. Make the pastry Rub the butter into the flour, icing sugar and salt until it looks like coarse breadcrumbs — or pulse in a food processor. Add the egg yolk and water and bring together into a dough without kneading it.

2. Chill the dough — 30 minutes Flatten into a disc, wrap, and refrigerate until firm.

3. Line the tin — 20 minutes chilling Roll out to about 3mm and line the tart tin, leaving the excess hanging over the edge. Prick the base, then chill again — this is what stops shrinkage.

4. Blind bake — 15 minutes Heat the oven to 180°C / 350°F. Line the shell with baking paper, fill with beans or rice, and bake until the edges look dry and pale gold.

5. Bake the base — 10 minutes Remove the paper and beans and bake until the base is sandy and fully cooked. Drop the oven to 140°C / 285°F.

6. Make the filling Whisk the eggs with the caster sugar until just combined — don't beat in air. Stir in the lemon juice, zest and cream, then strain through a fine sieve and skim off any foam.

7. Fill and bake — 30–35 minutes Pour the filling into the warm shell (easiest done with the tin already on the oven shelf). Bake until set with a faint wobble in the centre.

8. Cool and trim — 1 hour Cool completely in the tin, then trim the overhanging pastry with a serrated knife for a clean edge. Dust with icing sugar just before serving.

Notes

  • Straining the filling is the single biggest difference between a good tart and a great one — it catches the stringy bits of egg and gives you that glassy texture.
  • For a caramelised top, dust the cooled tart with caster sugar and blowtorch it.
  • Serve at room temperature, not fridge-cold, or the flavour flattens out.
  • Short on time? A shop-bought sweet shortcrust case works fine — blind bake it and jump to step 6.
